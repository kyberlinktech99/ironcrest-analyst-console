// KEY CONCEPTS
DMZ (DEMILITARIZED ZONE)
A network segment between the internet and the internal network. Public-facing servers (web, mail, DNS) live here. If compromised, attackers can't directly reach internal systems — they're still blocked by the inner firewall.
PERIMETER VS INTERNAL FIREWALL
The perimeter firewall controls internet-to-DMZ traffic. The internal firewall controls DMZ-to-internal traffic. Two firewalls means an attacker must compromise both to reach internal systems from the internet.
NETWORK SEGMENTATION
Dividing a network into isolated zones limits lateral movement. A compromised device in one segment can't directly attack devices in another — they must pass through a firewall or router with rules.
IDS VS IPS PLACEMENT
An IDS (detection only) is placed out-of-band — it monitors a copy of traffic. An IPS (blocks attacks) must be inline — traffic flows through it. Wrong placement defeats the purpose of the control.
