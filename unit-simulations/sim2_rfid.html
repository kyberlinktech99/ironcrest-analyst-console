RFID Vulnerability Lab
Most office buildings use RFID access cards. What most people don't know: standard 125kHz proximity cards broadcast your unique ID in plaintext — to anyone with a reader, up to 3 feet away. Let's understand how this attack works and how to defend against it.

// KEY CONCEPTS
RFID CLONING ATTACK
Reading a 125kHz card's ID wirelessly and writing it to a blank card. Requires no physical access to the card — just proximity. Tools like the Flipper Zero or custom readers can do this in seconds.
REPLAY ATTACK
Capturing a valid credential transmission and replaying it later to gain unauthorized access. Works on any system where the credential doesn't change (non-cryptographic RFID, static tokens).
MUTUAL AUTHENTICATION
Both parties verify each other's identity before access is granted. In RFID: the card proves it's legitimate to the reader, AND the reader proves it's legitimate to the card. Prevents cloning attacks.
DEFENSE IN DEPTH — PHYSICAL
No single control is sufficient. RFID + PIN (multi-factor physical authentication), camera (detective), security culture (administrative), and encrypted cards (technical) work together.
