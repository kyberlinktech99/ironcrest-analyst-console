// KEY CONCEPTS
EVIL TWIN ATTACK
Rogue AP broadcasts the same SSID as a legitimate network. Devices auto-connect based on signal strength or remembered networks. Attacker gains MITM position. Defense: VPN on public Wi-Fi, 802.1X authentication.
DEAUTHENTICATION ATTACK
802.11 management frames are unauthenticated — anyone can send forged deauth frames that force devices to disconnect. Used to disrupt service (DoS) or force reconnection to an evil twin. Defense: 802.11w (Protected Management Frames).
ROGUE ACCESS POINT
An unauthorized AP connected to the corporate network — often installed by an employee for convenience. Bypasses all perimeter security. Defense: wireless IDS, 802.1X port authentication, regular wireless surveys.
WPA3 vs WPA2
WPA3 uses Simultaneous Authentication of Equals (SAE) instead of WPA2's Pre-Shared Key — resistant to offline dictionary attacks. WPA3 also includes Protected Management Frames by default, preventing deauth attacks.
