Authentication Factor Builder
Authentication factors fall into three categories. MFA requires at least two DIFFERENT categories. Four scenarios — classify the factors and determine whether each login attempt constitutes true MFA or just multiple passwords.

// KEY CONCEPTS
WHAT MAKES REAL MFA
MFA requires factors from at least two DIFFERENT categories. Password + PIN = not MFA (both are "something you know"). Password + TOTP code = MFA (know + have). The AP exam specifically tests this distinction.
TOTP (TIME-BASED OTP)
A 6-digit code generated by an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy) that changes every 30 seconds. Requires possession of the registered device — classified as "something you have."
HARDWARE TOKENS
Physical devices (YubiKey, RSA SecurID) that generate or store cryptographic credentials. Classified as "something you have." Harder to phish than TOTP codes because they require physical presence.
BIOMETRICS AS AUTHENTICATION
Biometrics are "something you are" — fingerprint, face, retina, voice. They can't be forgotten or stolen the same way passwords can, but they also can't be changed if compromised. Best used as one layer in MFA, not standalone.
