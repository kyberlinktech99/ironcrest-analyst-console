// KEY CONCEPTS
SIEM CORRELATION
A SIEM aggregates logs from many sources and applies correlation rules to detect patterns that individual systems can't see. One failed login is noise; 500 failed logins in 30 seconds is a brute force alert. SIEMs detect patterns across time and sources.
TRUE POSITIVE vs FALSE POSITIVE
True Positive (TP): real attack, alert fired correctly. False Positive (FP): benign activity, alert fired incorrectly. Security teams tune SIEM rules to reduce FPs without missing TPs. Alert fatigue from too many FPs is a real operational risk.
DLP (DATA LOSS PREVENTION)
DLP systems monitor for sensitive data leaving the organization — through email, USB, cloud uploads, or web traffic. They can alert on or block transfers that match patterns (SSN, credit card numbers, classification labels). Used to enforce data classification policy.
SIEM + SOAR
SIEM detects. SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response) automates the response. When SIEM fires a brute force alert, SOAR can automatically block the IP, disable the account, and notify the analyst — reducing response time from hours to seconds.
