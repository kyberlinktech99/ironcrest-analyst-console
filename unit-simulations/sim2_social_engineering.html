Social Engineering Response
Four real workplace scenarios — tailgating, vishing, pretexting, and impersonation. For each one, choose the BEST response. Not just any response — the one that follows proper security protocol while remaining professional.
// SCORING
✅ Best response — full points. Follows protocol exactly.
⚠️ Acceptable response — partial points. Technically safe but not ideal.
❌ Poor response — zero points. Creates a security risk.
// KEY CONCEPTS
TAILGATING
Unauthorized access gained by following an authorized person through a controlled entry. Prevented by mantraps, security culture, and challenging strangers.
PRETEXTING
Creating a fabricated scenario to manipulate a target into providing information or access. The "pretext" is the invented cover story.
VISHING
Voice phishing — using phone calls to socially engineer targets. Attackers exploit authority, urgency, and helpfulness to bypass verification procedures.
VERIFICATION PROTOCOL
The correct response to any unusual access request: verify the person's identity through an independent channel, not through the channel the attacker is using.
URGENCY AS A RED FLAG
Urgency is the attacker's most powerful tool — it pressures victims into skipping verification. In security, the more urgent the request seems, the more carefully you should verify it.
SECURITY CULTURE
An organization where every employee feels empowered to challenge strangers and question unusual requests without fear of embarrassment. The most effective defense against social engineering.
