Data Classification Lab
Data classification determines what protections data requires. Three organizational scenarios — classify each data item into the correct tier, then answer a compliance or handling question. Classification errors cause both over-protection (wasted resources) and under-protection (breaches).

// KEY CONCEPTS
WHY CLASSIFY DATA?
Classification drives protection decisions — encryption requirements, access controls, retention periods, disposal methods, and regulatory compliance. Without classification, organizations either over-protect (expensive) or under-protect (breaches).
PII vs PHI vs PCI
PII (Personally Identifiable Information) — any data that identifies a person. PHI (Protected Health Information) — medical data, regulated by HIPAA. PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry) — cardholder data, regulated by card networks. All require Restricted classification.
DATA AT REST vs IN TRANSIT
Confidential and Restricted data must be encrypted both at rest (stored on disk) and in transit (moving across networks). Public data requires neither. Internal data typically requires encryption in transit only.
PRINCIPLE OF LEAST PRIVILEGE
Access to data should be limited to those who need it for their job function. Restricted data requires formal access requests, logging, and periodic access reviews. Even authorized users should only access what they need when they need it.
