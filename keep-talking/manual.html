Classify the request

Piggybacking: manipulating an authorized person into knowingly admitting someone. Tailgating: following an authorized person without that person’s awareness. Shoulder surfing: observing sensitive information as someone uses it.

Authority: claimed power or instructions from someone in power. Urgency: pressure from a short deadline. Consensus: pressure that everyone else complies. Scarcity: limited supply. Familiarity: claimed personal connection.

A number, link or identity claim supplied by the visitor is not an independent verification channel. Suspicion does not prove criminal intent or successful compromise.

Communication check: “Read me the request verbatim. Did the employee knowingly admit anyone? Which credential fields are present or missing?”

C / Risk assessment model

Risk links a threat, an exploitable vulnerability and a valuable asset. Record the supporting evidence and projected harm. Use the latest evidence, preserve earlier assessments, and separate likelihood from impact.

Likelihood scale — use the strongest current matching condition
RatingExercise condition
1 / LowAll relevant access doors latched and restricted; no bypass evidence.
2 / MediumAccess depends on intermittent failure or a door being left unlatched; downstream path is passable.
3 / HighCurrent evidence confirms a continuously open exterior entry and passable path to the asset.

Snapshot rule: a single open-door observation can identify a possible path, but it does not by itself confirm continuous access. Evidence Update 1 provides a tested condition.

Impact scale — evaluate the asset and consequence
RatingExercise condition
1 / LowRoutine, replaceable training materials; limited harm.
2 / MediumInternal operational material; recoverable disruption.
3 / HighReadable sensitive client records exposed, or essential payroll service suffers prolonged outage.

Exercise score = likelihood × impact. Higher score gets priority; a tie requires discussion. This ordinal classroom model is a simplification, not a required CED formula or a dollar estimate.

Evidence Update 2: Inventory Correction

When corrected contents change the projected harm but no access condition changes, revise impact and preserve likelihood under this exercise’s fixed door-based rules. In real assessments, asset value and adversary motivation can also affect likelihood.

D / Fund a layered defense

The Operator has current prices and the procurement menu. Your job is to match functions to the observed vulnerabilities. Meet all required outcomes within budget. Never obstruct emergency egress.

Mitigate: reduce likelihood or impact. Avoid: stop the risky activity. Transfer: shift a financial burden, such as through insurance. Accept: acknowledge a risk within tolerance.

Defense in depth: complementary layers reduce reliance on a single safeguard. Residual risk remains if people ignore procedures, locks are bypassed, or a control fails. Explain both the value of your plan and its limits.

Read-back: “Our plan addresses ______ through ______. If that fails, ______ still limits harm. We will monitor ______.”
